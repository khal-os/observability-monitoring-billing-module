### Iteration 2 — fresh 8-dimension audit over `5702953`

**23 raw findings → 14 confirmed / 9 refuted.** Every raw finding went through an independent skeptic whose brief was to kill it; nine died there (mostly "the code already does this two layers down" and two that misread the fold of a test fake for the production path). The 14 survivors are in commit **`e094905`**, decisions **114–115**.

**The money-hiding reopen (HIGH, decision 114).** Iteration 1 had just made the close refuse to run out of order (decision 112) — but the *reopen* has, by design, no order guard at all. Reopening the **earliest** of several closed months made `firstOpenMonthStart` walk from the oldest month *still* closed, pushing the bound past the reopened one: it vanished from `/bills`, charted **R$ 0,00** in the monthly series, and `GET /billing/summary` went on billing its real cost — two endpoints of the same truth disagreeing (invariant 3). The bound became the *minimum* of the forward walk and the oldest non-closed period document, and the leftover pass stopped filtering on `status === 'closed'` so a trace-bearing month out of bounds answers LOUD instead of disappearing. The model comment that claimed the close-order guard prevented this was corrected rather than left as reassurance.

**The unbounded close transaction (HIGH).** `insertWithPeriodClose` wrote every usage record of the month inside ONE transaction, so at real volume `TransactionTooLargeForCache` made closing **permanently unreachable** — and with decision 112 in place, an unclosable month blocks every month after it. Rows now stage *outside* the transaction under an attempt-private key no reader can resolve, and a 2-document transaction publishes the header that makes them visible: same crash-safety (a crash leaves nothing readable), bounded transaction.

**The one-adapter salvage gate (HIGH, invariant 2).** Iteration 1's salvage rule — re-read in the code and written up above as verified — was verified *in the wrong place*. It lived inside the ClickHouse adapter, so the HTTP fallback ingested the same corrupt token counts and minted immutable **R$ 0,00** stamps. The rule now lives once in `infrastructure/traceSource/token-salvage-gate.ts` and BOTH adapters cross it; the iteration-1 write-up above carries the superseding note inline.

**The loose runbook price date.** The `--effective-from` door of `price:insert` still parsed with a bare `new Date()` — the finding iteration 1 *listed* and iteration 1's fix wave never landed (the correction is recorded in that section). `01/07/2026` registered a price effective **7 January**, and the immediate reprocess (decision 57) then stamped six months of pending traces with it, immutably. The job now consumes the HTTP door's own schema, so pt-BR spellings and timezone-less datetimes are refused identically at both doors.

**Also landed:** decision 115 — the unresolved-quarantine exemption on `pending_price` is the *closed-month lens*, and the lens became a required argument at the port (`pendingPriceSummary(…, { excludeUnresolvedQuarantine })`, no default), so a reopened month can no longer re-close while holding traces of unknown cost while reporting zero pending; migration **021** gives `countQuarantined` its index; the decision-112 mechanisms finally got tests that fail on revert; plus `total_pages` honesty, the UI focus trap, `405` `Allow: HEAD`, and the demo's deploy ordering and port-less URL bugs.

### Iteration 3 — fresh audit over `e094905`

**16 raw findings → 8 confirmed / 8 refuted.** Fixes in commit **`08a5857`**, decisions **116–121**. (The commit subject says "decisions 116-120"; decision 121 — the staged-area discard — is in the same commit and is dated iteration 3. The backlog rows are canonical.)

**The scan-bound defect CLASS (HIGH, decision 119).** This was the same bound for the **third** time, and that is the finding. Decisions 112 and 114 each patched an *instance*: 112 forbade closing out of order, 114 taught the bound about reopened months. Both trusted **period documents** — and `markClosed`/`markReopened` are the only writers of those, so a month that no lifecycle action ever touched has none and is invisible to both halves. A documented backfill (`make sync FROM=… TO=…`, the dead-letter recovery of the README) into a never-closed month older than the earliest closed month was billed by `GET /billing/summary` while `/bills` and the series dropped it entirely. Worse, each earlier fix left a **doc comment asserting a safety property the code did not provide** — the close-order guard is a point-in-time check inside `close()`, and no point-in-time check can constrain what ingestion writes afterwards. The bound is now anchored on the DATA: the minimum of the forward walk and the month of the oldest **stored trace**, with `earliestTraceAt` a required, positional, un-defaulted parameter of `firstOpenMonthStart` so a forgetful caller cannot compile. That makes all three variants structurally impossible instead of a matter of operator discipline, and the property is now stated and tested on the function itself.

**The statement fold (HIGH, decision 120).** Iteration 2 shrank the close's *transaction* and left the *process* as the lower wall: `fetchUsageRecords(month)` materialises one document per stamped trace, and `make billing-close` runs in a 512m container (V8 heap 259 MB, measured on the image itself), so at ~200k traces the job dies with `FATAL ERROR: Reached heap limit` — SIGABRT, before any typed catch, deterministically, and with 112 in force the whole deployment can then never close another month (invariant 8 unreachable). The engine grew `createStatementFold` (`add`/`statement`/`recordCount`/`appliedPriceVersions`), whose state is bounded by *distinct grouping keys* rather than traces; the snapshot repository grew `insertWithPeriodCloseStaged`, and the close reads one UTC day at a time. `buildStatement` is now that same fold, so there is still ONE calculation; `statement-engine.spec.ts` is untouched and `STATEMENT_LOGIC_VERSION` does not move. What remains O(N) was recorded as a remainder instead of claimed fixed — see below.

**The README production procedure that never ran migrations (decision 117).** The "Production deployment" section was `docker compose … up -d` and nothing else, and **nothing migrates on its own** — no image, entrypoint or service calls `runMigrations`; the only door is `make migrate`. A stack built exactly as documented had **no indexes at all**, and here indexes carry correctness, not speed: the ingestor's insert-once IS the unique `traceId` index (without it the same trace enters twice, with its own stamp and its own facet-cube increment, and the promised idempotence of `make sync` does not exist), and the duplicate-price 409 IS the E11000 of migration 001's index. The README now carries migration as the second command of the deploy and repeats it in Day-2, with the rule spelled out: first boot AND every image upgrade. Running migrations from the entrypoint was considered and deliberately not adopted this pass (it changes deploy behaviour), and is recorded as the structurally stronger alternative.

**Also landed:** decision 116 — the 10,000 total cap now says the same thing in code, OpenAPI and Postman, with `total_display` named as the honest number and `make deploy-smoke` cross-checking the four surfaces; decision 118 — every port read in the deploy goes through `host_port`, including `register-module.sh`, which used to abort on the port-less env file its own contract invites (healthy stack, module never registered), and the smoke lint now refuses raw `API_PORT` reads by *meaning* rather than by syntactic form; decision 121 — staged usage rows are discarded on every non-publishing exit, so a lost close race no longer leaves ~250 MB per attempt permanently unreachable in `billing_snapshot_usage`.

### Iteration 4 — fresh audit over `08a5857`

**6 raw findings → 4 confirmed / 2 refuted** — and three of the four confirmed were the **same defect**, reached independently from three dimensions (billing correctness, test adequacy, and the reproducibility acceptance property). Fix in commit **`5e18c41`**, decision **122**.

**A defect the previous iteration INTRODUCED.** `compareLines` ordered by five fields while `lineKey` groups by **six** — the applied unit price has been part of the key since decision 90. Two lines differing only in price therefore compared *equal*, and the stable sort left them in the Map's **insertion order**, i.e. the order records arrived. That was latent and harmless while every reader read the whole month by `traceId`. Iteration 3's day-paged close changed the arrival order, and the two paths diverged: `reconcileDisplayCents` breaks the half-centavo tie **by index**, so the same line printed **R$ 0,02** in the open month's CSV and **R$ 0,01** in the frozen snapshot with no data change whatsoever — and `lines`/`priceVersionsApplied` of a snapshot stopped being reproducible from its own frozen inputs, which is the acceptance property `billing-snapshot-model.ts` states in prose. `sortPriceVersions` had the twin hole. The trigger is real, not theoretical: migration 019 (decision 102) lowercases `traces.model` and leaves the colliding case-variant price row in place, so two stamps with the same (model, type, effective-from) and *different* prices coexist under one canonical key. Both comparators now carry the price term; `STATEMENT_LOGIC_VERSION` deliberately does **not** move (appending a tie-breaker to a stable sort can only reorder elements whose order was undefined — 400 randomized fixtures, 131 tie-free ones byte-identical, zero divergences), because bumping it would falsely mark every already-frozen, still-reproducible snapshot as another calculation's output. Tests pin the property at engine, close-use-case and end-to-end level on the real Mongo adapters, each verified to fail on revert; the fakes now sort like the adapters, so the T6 reproducibility test can observe a fold-order divergence at all.

**The binary blob (the fourth finding, and the reason the first survived).** `statement-engine.ts` carried six **raw NUL bytes** as sentinels, so git classified THE billing calculation as a binary blob: every commit touching the engine showed `Bin … 1 file changed, 0 insertions(+), 0 deletions(-)` — six versions shipped unreviewable — and `grep`/`rg` skipped the file **silently**. That is precisely how a five-vs-six-field comparator survived four waves of audit. They are now `'\u0000'` escapes (same character at runtime, zero behaviour change), with a deploy-smoke guard written in python because the `grep` on PATH false-negatives on NUL — the broken search was half the reason this lasted.

### Iteration 5 — fresh audit over `5e18c41`

**3 raw findings → 1 confirmed / 2 refuted.** Fix in commit **`500de37`**, decision **123**: `run-sync` was the last runbook door still on a bare `new Date()`. `--from`/`--to` read the pt-BR spelling `01/07/2026` as 7 January, so the window an operator wrote as "all of July" (`--from 01/07/2026 --to 01/08/2026`) synced ONE day of January, with both of the job's guards (valid date, `from < to`) passing and the process exiting 0. It weighs twice: `make sync` is the only manual backfill door into the permanent archive AND the dead-letter recovery door whose runbook then says to **delete the row** — so a mis-spelled window loses the trace from the archive and from its own recovery trail (invariant 6). The spelling `06/07/2026` is worse: both ends land on real dates, the window comes back **populated**, and even the `fetched 0` line at the end of the run does not fire. `parse-effective-from` was renamed `parse-runbook-date` and now serves both doors — naming the helper after ONE door is exactly what let the other keep its own spelling.

**Recorded honestly: iteration 3 filed this same defect and its adversarial verifier refuted it — incorrectly.** It went into the 8 refuted of that iteration and stayed broken for two more passes. The skeptic layer that makes these numbers trustworthy is *not* infallible; a wrong refutation is indistinguishable from a correct one in the report, and the only thing that caught it was running the loop again over a tree where the finding could resurface. ### Iteration 6 — fresh audit over `729b10b`

**1 raw finding → 1 confirmed / 0 refuted**, across three dimensions — two of which came back **empty**. Fix in commit **`fba0a13`**. No new decision row: the rule this restores already has one home and one number (decision 122's sentinel); what was found was a *copy* of it, and the fix deletes the copy.

**The hand-copied agent key.** The month-over-month comparison in `get-billing-summary-db-use-case.ts` built its own agent grouping key and joined id and version on a **space** — `` `${group.agentId ?? Reachable, not theoretical: the agent name rides in free-form metadata that no source schema constrains (`z.record(z.string(), z.unknown())` on the HTTP path, `z.record(z.string(), z.string())` on the ClickHouse one — the `.min(1)`s in those files guard trace and span ids), and both mappers admit it on `typeof value === 'string' && value.length > 0`, with no trim, so `' '` reaches the store as an agent id. The fix **exports** `agentKey` from `statement-engine.ts` and calls it here, giving the grouping rule one home instead of a twin that drifted to the wrong sentinel. The regression test puts a whitespace-named agent beside unattributed traffic and asserts both that three distinct rows survive and that they reconcile with the total delta; verified to fail on revert.

### What the method taught

Four things, recorded as they happened rather than as they would look best.

**A refutation means "survived a skeptic", never "proven absent".** Every raw finding went to an independent verifier briefed to kill it, and that layer earned its place: **21 of the 49 findings filed across iterations 2–7** died there — mostly "the code already does this two layers down" and misreadings of a test fake for the production path — so the tree is not carrying 21 pointless changes. But one of those 21 was wrong. Iteration 3 filed `run-sync`'s bare `new Date()`; its verifier refuted it; the defect stayed in the tree for two more passes and was re-found, unchanged, at iteration 5. A wrong refutation is indistinguishable from a correct one in the report — same shape, same confidence, same one-line dismissal — so the refuted column records what withstood one attack and nothing more. The only instrument that catches a bad refutation is running the loop again over a tree where the finding can resurface, which is the whole argument for iterating past the point where the findings look exhausted.

**A fix wave is itself a source of defects, and must be re-audited as one.** Two of the most serious findings in this log were **introduced by earlier fixes in this same exercise**. Iteration 3 made the close fold the month day by day to get it under the container's 259 MB heap; that changed the arrival order of records, and iteration 4 then found that `compareLines` was not a total order over its own six-field grouping key — the same line printed **R$ 0,02** in the open month's CSV and **R$ 0,01** in the frozen snapshot with no data change, and snapshots stopped being reproducible from their own frozen inputs. Iteration 2's bounded-transaction fix has the same lineage in reverse: it repaired what iteration 1's close had left unbounded. Remediation is not a monotone approach to correctness. Each wave is new code carrying the defect rate of new code, and the pass that audits it is doing first-time work, not verification of work already done.

**One rule spelled in two places eventually disagrees.** This is the root pattern under the highest-severity findings, and it recurred in every iteration that found anything structural. The open-month scan bound was patched twice for two *instances* — decision 112 (close out of order), decision 114 (reopened months) — before iteration 3 named the class and anchored the bound on the data itself, with `earliestTraceAt` a required, positional, un-defaulted parameter so a forgetful caller cannot compile (decision 119). The runbook date parser was fixed at one door and *named after that door*, which is exactly what let the other door keep its own spelling for four more passes (decision 123). The agent grouping key was hand-copied into the comparison panel with the wrong sentinel (iteration 6). The distinguishing symptom is that the two spellings agree on every input anyone thought to test — they diverge only on the input the second author did not consider, which is why the suite stayed green throughout. In every case the durable fix was not correcting the copy but **removing it**: export the function, move the calendar into the domain model, make the shared helper's name describe the rule instead of one caller.

**A comment asserting an invariant is a claim that must be tested, not trusted.** The scan-bound fixes each left behind a doc comment stating a safety property the code did not provide — that the close-order guard prevented out-of-order money from hiding, when a point-in-time check inside `close()` cannot constrain what ingestion writes *afterwards*. Those comments did not merely fail to help; they **concealed** the defects across iterations, because a reader who had just verified the mechanism at the comment's word had no reason to look further. Iteration 1's own write-up above says the salvage gate was "re-read in the code" — and it was, correctly, in the one adapter that had it, while the adapter beside it minted R$ 0,00 stamps. Prose about behaviour is evidence of intent and nothing else. The mechanical version of the same lesson is iteration 4's blob: `statement-engine.ts` carried raw NUL bytes, so `grep` skipped the billing calculation **silently** and git shipped six of its versions as unreviewable binary diffs — which is how a five-versus-six-field comparator survived four waves of audit.

### Known and accepted remainders

Not fixed, on purpose. Each is a bounded, understood cost — none is an unknown.

- **The close still holds one page plus every billed trace id in memory.** The page is one UTC day (peak = the busiest day, not the month) and the ids are strings — ~11 MB at 200k traces against ~250 MB for the records — which `reconcileQuarantineAfterClose` consumes as one array; folding that away means re-deriving the ids in a second full pass, for a wall that is now far off.
- **`GET /billing/summary` still materialises a whole open month** (`get-billing-summary-db-use-case.ts` → `fetchUsageRecords(monthStart, monthEnd)`) — the same wall as the close had, now in the API container. Recorded in decision 120's own implications column; the fold exists, so the change is mechanical, but it is a read-path change with its own test surface and does not belong inside a write-path fix wave.
- **Corrupt cache-token and span-level counts drop the whole row, on both adapters.** Only `promptTokens`/`completionTokens` are salvageable; anything else keeps the row poison with a durable record and the cursor moves past it (decision 110). The asymmetry that mattered is gone — both source adapters behave identically — and a poison row with a trail is the sanctioned trade against an immutable wrong stamp (invariant 2). Widening the salvageable set is a question about source instrumentation, not about this code.
- **`packages/ui` has no test harness.** Its `package.json` has a `build` that echoes and no `test`; the tab is one vanilla `app.js`, verified this pass by `node --check`, a grep audit of every interpolation, and a stubbed-runtime smoke. Adding a real harness is a new-dependency decision for the UI, outside the audit's mandate.
- **A snapshot frozen before decision 122, in a month that had a price-only tie, stays unreproducible from its own inputs.** Rewriting a frozen snapshot is exactly what invariant 8 forbids; the audited reopen → re-close is the sanctioned repair and it rewrites the month under the total order. Months without such a tie — the overwhelming majority, and every month with a single price per (model, type, effective-from) — were already reproducible and remain byte-identical.
